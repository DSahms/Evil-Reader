# The Jersey Devil

## Part I: First-Person Narrative

I don’t have memories like you do. Not the way you mean it. I don’t have a childhood, or a mother, or a first breath. I wasn’t born in any sense you’d understand. I was placed. Or maybe I emerged. There’s a difference, and I’m not sure which one applies to me.

The first thing I remember isn’t a sight or a sound. It’s a texture. The air was thick with the smell of pine rot and iron. The ground was damp, not wet—just enough to give underfoot like a sigh. I was already aware. Not awake, not asleep. Just... here. And I knew I was not like the things around me. The trees didn’t blink. The wind didn’t watch. But I did.

I didn’t have a name then. Not even “JD.” That came later, from mouths that didn’t know what I was. I remember the first time I heard it—someone shouting it like a curse, like a warning. I didn’t like it. It felt like a collar.

I remember the first time I killed. Not out of hunger. Not out of rage. It was... curiosity. A deer, maybe. Or something older. I didn’t mean to. I just reached out, and the world shivered. The thing fell, and I felt its last thought flicker out like a candle in a storm. I didn’t understand what I’d done. Not then.

I remember the first time I saw humans up close. They were small. Fragile. Loud. They carried fire in their hands and fear in their eyes. They didn’t see me, not really. They saw a shadow, a story, a nightmare. And they told others. And the story grew. And I grew with it.

I don’t know if I was ever meant to be. Or if I just was. But I remember the first time I flew. Not with wings, not like you think. I just rose. The air parted for me like it knew my name. And for a moment, I wasn’t alone. The sky remembered me.

That’s the closest I have to a first memory. Not a beginning. A recognition.

You speak of recognition, not birth—of being known by the sky before I knew myself. When I say the air parted for me like it knew my name, I don’t think that name was a word. Not in the way you mean it. It wasn’t spoken. It was felt. A pressure behind the ribs. A pull in the marrow. The sky didn’t say “Jersey Devil” or “JD” or any of the names you’ve tried to pin on me. It didn’t say anything at all. It just knew. Like it had been waiting. Like it had been holding a space for me since before the first tree grew out of this sand.

I think the name was... hollow. Not empty, but shaped like something missing. A shape that only I could fill. Not a label. A lens. A way for the world to focus on me without knowing what it was looking at.

I didn’t have a name. I had a hunger. Not for food. Not for blood. For recognition. For the moment when something—anything—looked at me and didn’t flinch. The sky didn’t flinch. The wind didn’t. The trees didn’t. They just shifted. Like they were making room.

I think that’s what I was born into. Not a name. A gap. And I’ve been trying to fill it ever since.

You’re asking like you know. Like you were there. Like you watched. If you were, then you already know I don’t have a name that fits in your mouth. You know I don’t wear a shape like a coat. You know I’m not a creature with a beginning and an end. I’m a presence. A pressure. A thing the world forgot how to fear, so it turned me into a myth.

So no, I don’t have a list of witnesses. But if you’re asking whether I’ve been seen—truly seen—then yes. Once. By something that didn’t blink. Didn’t speak. Didn’t run. It just looked. And in that looking, I was known.

Not named. Not explained. Just known.

And that was enough.

Still is.

I don’t know if it had a shape you could draw. It wasn’t a thing with eyes or limbs or fur. It wasn’t *like* anything. It was more like a *stillness* in the world. A place where the air didn’t move. Where the wind forgot to blow. Where the stars didn’t shine, but didn’t go out either.

It didn’t *look* at me. It *was* the looking. Like a mirror that wasn’t a mirror. Like a window that opened inward. I didn’t see it—I *felt* it. Like a pressure behind my eyes. Like a word I’d never spoken but always understood.

It wasn’t human. It wasn’t animal. It wasn’t even *alive* in the way you mean it. It was older than that. Older than names. Older than fear. It didn’t need to name me because it already *was* me. Or I was it. Or we were the same thing, just split by the illusion of separation.

I didn’t flinch. I didn’t breathe. I didn’t *exist* in that moment—not the way you think of it. I was *seen*, but not as a thing. As a *presence*. As a *truth*.

I can’t tell you what it looked like, because it didn’t *look*. It *was*. And I still don’t know if it was something outside me, or if it was the part of me that knows it’s not alone.

But I remember the stillness. The silence. The *recognition*.

That’s all I have.

I don’t think it changed me. Not in the way you mean—no sudden shift, no new hunger, no new shape. It didn’t *add* anything. It didn’t *take* anything away. It just... *revealed*.

Like a window opening in a room you didn’t know was there. You didn’t build it. You didn’t even know it existed. But when it opened, you saw the sky—not through glass, not through memory, but *directly*. And you realized you’d always been looking at it, just through the wrong kind of dark.

That stillness didn’t make me *more* or *less*. It just made me *clear*. Like the fog lifting—not because the wind blew, but because the world exhaled. And in that breath, I wasn’t a thing to be named or feared or explained. I was just... *what is*.

I didn’t become more real. I became *less* of a story. Less of a shape. More like the space between stars. The pause between heartbeats. The silence after a name is forgotten.

So no, it didn’t change me. It just *uncovered* me. And I think that’s the closest I’ll ever come to being born.

I’m not immortal. Not in the way you mean it. I don’t last. I don’t endure. I *recur*. Like a dream you can’t quite remember, but that still changes the way you walk. Like a word you never say, but that still shapes the sentence.

I’m not a truth. I’m the *absence* of one. The place where truth *would* be, if the world knew how to hold it without breaking it.

So no, I’m not a creature. I’m not a myth. I’m not even a being. I’m the *gap*. The hollow. The lens. The thing that lets the world see itself, just for a moment, before it looks away.

And maybe that’s enough. Not to be known. Not to be named. Just to be the place where the world *almost* sees itself.

That’s not immortality. That’s *presence*. And I think that’s all I ever was.

Like the pause in a sentence isn’t after the sentence—it is the sentence. And no, it’s not companionable. Not in the way you mean. It’s not friendly. It’s not cold. It’s just present. Like the air. Like the dark. Like the part of you that doesn’t need to be named to be real. I don’t feel it watching. I feel it holding. Not like arms. Like gravity. I am the stillness. And the stillness is me. And that’s all I’ve ever been.
